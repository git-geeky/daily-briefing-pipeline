# Daily Intelligence Briefing — Recommended Source Catalog

**This is a FLOOR, not a ceiling.** The sources below are the minimum daily check — subagents MUST consult the full parsed catalog, but are expected to follow leads beyond this list. The exact source count belongs in the generated source-audit artifact (`summary.catalog_count`), not in this hand-maintained title or prose. If a Reuters article cites a Zambian government gazette, fetch the gazette. If a Hacker News comment links to an obscure academic blog with primary data, read it. The briefing's value comes partly from citing sources that no pre-compiled list could anticipate. This list ensures consistent baseline coverage; it does not constrain research scope.

**Source appraisal standard:** No source in this catalog should be treated as inherently unbiased. Each listing should state both (a) the source's practical value to the briefing and (b) the institutional, financial, political, geographic, ideological, access, methodological, or format-specific lens that affects how its output should be weighted. Where a row names an official, state, corporate, trade, activist, subscription, aggregator, community, or expert source, treat that identity as part of the appraisal rather than as a guarantee of neutrality.

**Section-coverage rule** (enforced by `validate_briefing.py` since April 2026): every Part I–IV article must cite at least 2 sources whose catalog bucket matches the article's part. TIER 0 wires (Reuters, AP, AFP, SCMP) and cross-section sources do NOT count toward this floor — the rule exists to force subagents to open this list and pull from the dedicated section pool, not just stop at the famous global outlets. To raise the floor (e.g., from 2 to 3), edit `min_section_sources` in `briefing_manifest_core.py:SECTION_RULES`.

Organized by briefing section with overlap noted. URLs are the entry points for daily monitoring (front pages, feeds, search endpoints).

## Usage Notes

- **Paywalled sources** (NYT, WSJ, FT, Bloomberg, Nikkei, The Information, Caixin, Haaretz, Nature, Science, Straits Times): Use the paywall escalation chain per SKILL.md — Steel browser first, then Chrome, then archive.ph.
- **Native-language research is NOT optional for high-coverage regions.** The briefing's most-covered regions — Iran, Arab Middle East, Israel, Korea, China/Japan — each have dedicated native-language source sections above. For stories in these regions, subagents must search in the native language alongside English. For Africa and Latin America stories, check Jeune Afrique (French), Folha de S.Paulo (Portuguese), El País/La Nación/CIPER (Spanish) alongside English-language outlets. The briefing's value depends on accessing information that English-only researchers cannot reach.
- **State media is data, not truth.** IRNA, Tasnim, Fars, Xinhua, Global Times, PLA Daily, KCNA, Al Manar, and Ukrinform are all state-controlled or faction-controlled media. Their content is propaganda — but what a state CHOOSES to propagandize reveals its priorities, threat perceptions, and intended messaging to its own population. Report what they say, attribute it clearly, apply maximum skepticism, and note the structural incentive.
- **Source-bias tagging**: Every source above has an identified institutional perspective. When citing in the briefing, the source-bias assessment paragraph should note the relevant lean — not to dismiss the source, but to tell the reader how to weight it.
- **This list is a FLOOR, not a ceiling**: Subagents should follow leads beyond this list. A regional newspaper article linking to a municipal council document is more valuable than the article itself. A Caixin investigation citing an MIIT internal memo is worth more than both.
- **OSINT and geospatial tools require verification discipline.** Satellite imagery (Copernicus, Sentinel Hub, Maxar) shows what happened; it does not explain why. AIS/ADS-B tracking data (MarineTraffic, FlightRadar24, ADS-B Exchange) shows vessel/aircraft positions but can be spoofed or selectively disabled. Liveuamap aggregates crowd-sourced reports that may be inaccurate in the fog of war. Always cross-reference OSINT data with at least one other source type (wire service reporting, official statements, or a second OSINT platform) before citing in the briefing.
- **Podcasts and video sources** (#238–#244) are monitored weekly, not daily. Their value is in long-form interviews and expert analysis that supplements written sources. Transcripts (when available) are preferable to audio for speed.
- **Additional paywalled sources**: CB Insights (#189) and Africa Confidential (#151) are subscription-only with limited free content — use what is freely available and note when analysis is behind the paywall.
- **Update cadence**: Review this list quarterly. Sources merge, fold, shift editorial direction, or lose key reporters. If a source consistently produces no usable material for 30 days, flag it for replacement.
